Qigong and neigong classes in Toronto and online

A Saturday neigong class at 255 Broadview Ave in Riverdale, in person and live online. Three to five movements per class, each one coached.

Toronto Qigong is a neigong school on Broadview Avenue in Riverdale. We teach people how to build an internal state through movement and how to return to it. The group meets Saturday mornings, and private lessons run through the rest of the week for people who want closer coaching.

What happens in a class

A class covers three to five movements from a qigong form. Keeping the number small leaves time to coach the energetics of each movement: where the attention sits, what changes in the breath, what you feel in the hands and the spine, and how to call that state back later without the room, the music or the teacher.

You stand, move, stop and check, then repeat the movement with one thing adjusted. The aim is a few movements you can practise on your own during the week.

How we teach neigong

We map a state across the five senses. When a movement produces a state worth keeping, such as calm or alertness, we look at what you see, hear, feel, smell and taste while you are in it. The movement is then trained as the physical trigger for that state, so that with practice it becomes easier to return to.

We teach this in plain language, from Daoist philosophy and from what students notice in their own bodies. You do the work yourself and judge the results yourself.

Why people practise here

The school starts from a hard assumption. Every day, work, screens and other people's demands take some of your vitality, and neigong is the practice of taking some of it back. The Yinfu Jing, a Daoist text, describes heaven, earth, people and things all feeding on one another:

Classes draw on four classical sets: 八段錦 Eight Pieces of Brocade, 香功 Fragrant Qigong, 易筋經 the Sinew Changing Classic, and the Five Yin Organ Exercise. More on the forms.
