Toronto Qigong / What is neigong?

What is neigong?

內功 nèigōng, internal skill. How it relates to qigong, how we teach it, and the Daoist texts behind the practice.

內功 nèigōng means internal skill. Qigong 氣功 qìgōng means skill with qi. The two words overlap. At Toronto Qigong we teach neigong through qigong forms, with the class time spent on the internal side of each movement.

Neigong as we teach it

We teach neigong in three steps.

Produce the state. Slow, coached movement until you notice a change, such as feeling steadier, quieter or more alert.

Map the state. Name it through the five senses. What do you see when you are in it, what do you hear, what do you feel and where, is there a smell or a taste. Each person's map is a little different, and the map is what makes the state findable again.

Anchor the state. The movement is trained as the trigger for the state. Students practise the pairing between classes so it can be used outside the room.

The texts behind the practice

We teach from a philosophical place and use the classical sources directly. The Yinfu Jing opens with this line:

In class we apply it by watching what a movement does to you before deciding what it is for.

The same text is unsentimental about the world. It describes heaven and earth, people and things all taking from one another:

The school takes its stance from this passage. We assume the world takes vitality from you every day, and we train to take it back in proper measure so the three robberies stay balanced.

Scope of the training

The training is a practice of attention and state. Classes do not diagnose or treat any condition, and neigong is not a substitute for medical care.

Questions people ask

What does neigong mean?

內功 nèigōng means internal skill or internal work. Qigong 氣功 qìgōng means skill with qi. In practice neigong names the internal side of training.

Is neigong a martial art?

Neigong is trained in many martial lineages. It is also trained on its own as cultivation. We teach it on its own.

Do I have to believe in qi?

No. You need to pay attention to what happens in your body and check whether the state comes back when you call it.

What texts do you teach from?

Classes draw on Daoist sources including the Huangdi Yinfu Jing 黃帝陰符經. Every quotation we use is given in the original characters with pinyin and a gloss, and the text is named.
